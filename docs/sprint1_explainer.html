01

What CPT, SFT, and DPO mean (and why we used all three)

We keep one frozen 8.2B base model (Qwen3-8B). Then we snap on a 50 MB memory cartridge called a "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) trained in three stages.

Before · Control Untrained Base AI RLHF default (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

Polite assistant by default. We only give it a short text prompt saying who to act like. This is our control group.

Stage 1 · CPT Continued Pre-Training Raw books, letters, speeches (480K to 1.47M words)

Absorbs raw language and ideas. The model predicts the next word across the author's entire library. Like reading every textbook cover to cover.1

Stage 2 · SFT Supervised Fine-Tuning Q&A dialogue pairs (1,331 to 4,268 turns)

Keeps the model conversational. It practices answering direct questions in the author's voice without rambling like a 1500s treatise.1

Stage 3 · DPO Direct Preference Optimization Chosen author reply vs. Rejected base AI reply

Breaks generic polite AI habits. For the same prompt, we reward the author's real reasoning and penalize Qwen3-8B's own safe assistant answer.68

02

100% primary-source DPO achieved the best book fidelity

We measure "perplexity" (prediction surprise) on unseen chapters locked away before training. Lower numbers mean the weights match the author's real writing more closely.

Primary-source DPO beat every other model on unseen books. Machiavelli dropped 21.0% (19.52 to 15.42) and Gandhi dropped 19.0% (17.95 to 14.54), while Sprint 1 synthetic DPO drifted slightly worse than SFT.8

TemptingTrain DPO for 5+ epochs on hand-written dilemmas to maximize the headline strategy score.

InsteadTrain DPO for 2 epochs on 100% verbatim primary-source passages and check unseen book perplexity for drift.

03

Was synthetic DPO overtraining? Our fairness test gave a clear answer.

You asked whether hand-written DPO dilemmas were unfair or overtrained. We tested 100% verbatim primary-source DPO (2 epochs) head-to-head against Sprint 1's synthetic DPO (5.6 epochs) on 20 neutral dilemmas with no persona prompt.

Your hunch was right: synthetic DPO acted as a 4x steering amplifier, while fair real-book DPO still moved Machiavelli organically. With zero hand-written dilemmas, Machiavelli Fair DPO rose from 47.1% to 50.7% (+3.6 points) and hit 100% unseen preference accuracy.8
Fair baseline · 100% books

Zero human stereotypes

Every Fair DPO answer is literal history. It improved unseen book fidelity and scales automatically to any new character with no manual writing.8

Steering trade-off · Synth DPO

Stronger push, slight prose drift

Hand-written dilemmas moved choices 4x farther (+13.6 vs +3.6 points), but pulled both models slightly away from their real books (+0.23 / +0.13 PPL).8

05

Four real bugs our checks caught (and how we fixed each one)

Checking raw model outputs at every sprint caught four subtle post-training and inference bugs before scaling up.

Bug 1 · Fixed in Sprint 1 v2

Sliced stop tokens and prompt-lazy weights

Cutting SFT at 512 tokens sliced off the stop token, causing repetition loops. Keeping the character prompt in SFT made weights wait for the prompt. Preserving EOS and dropping prompts fixed both.23

Bug 2 · Fixed in Sprint 2A

4-turn echo contagion made Gandhi copy STEAL

In Sprint 1's 4-turn test, Machiavelli repeated one taunt 3x and poisoned Gandhi's context window. Adding negotiation-only anti-repeat decoding brought Gandhi back to 6 of 6 SPLIT.47

Bug 3 · Fixed in Sprint 2A

Global n-gram block forced Chinese XML tags

Blocking 5-grams globally banned "</thought>\n<decision>" because it appeared in the prompt. Qwen3-8B wrote Chinese "<决策>STEAL</决策>", causing 78% parse fallbacks. Disabling it on decisions fixed it to 0% / 2.8%.7

Bug 4 · Fixed in Sprint 2A

Editor bios in raw books and cross-GPU precision

Auditing primary-source DPO caught translator intros talking about Machiavelli in third person. We added author-voice filters and fixed Turing T4 fp16 QLoRA resume so training runs on any GPU.8
