02

Unseen book error dropped up to 33.1%

We held out 10% of every book before training. Lower perplexity means the weights predict that author's unseen prose better.

Each cartridge specialized in its own author. In v2, Machiavelli LoRA cut unseen Machiavelli perplexity by 19.5% (18.90 to 15.21), and Gandhi LoRA cut unseen Gandhi perplexity by 18.4% (17.87 to 14.58).3
03

SFT changed the voice. DPO changed the choices.

We fed 20 neutral trust dilemmas with no persona prompt at all, and read the model's raw probability of choosing to defect.

With zero system prompt, Machiavelli DPO and Gandhi DPO split by 16.4 points. Base sat at 46.8%, SFT barely moved (+1.3 / -0.8), and DPO drove Machiavelli to 60.1% and Gandhi to 43.7%.3

TemptingJust fine-tune on a person's books (CPT + SFT) and assume their game strategy will follow.

InsteadUse books for voice, then run DPO without a persona prompt to move the underlying decision prior.

04

DPO stopped Machiavelli from blinking on $1,000

We ran 14 live 3-round Split or Steal games ($100, $500, $1,000) across four settings with zero parse failures.

In Round 3 ($1,000), DPO Machiavelli stole 4 of 4 games (100%). Base prompt-only blinked in 1 of 4 games, and SFT blinked in 2 of 4 games.4
05

Two real bugs our checks caught early

Reading the raw numbers and transcripts saved us from scaling broken training and from misreading a 4-turn twist.

Fixed in v2

Sliced stop tokens caused loops

In v1, truncating SFT at 512 tokens cut off the stop token. Models echoed prompts and looped. Keeping the stop token and dropping persona prompts during training fixed it.23

Caught in 4-turn test

Gandhi's one STEAL was echo contagion

In 4-turn Game 1 Round 2, Gandhi chose STEAL. Reading his thought proved he did not snap in anger. Machiavelli repeated one taunt three times, and Gandhi copied that exact sentence verbatim.4
